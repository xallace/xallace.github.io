Incircle of a Triangle

The incenter M is found at the intersection of two angle bisectors (e.g. wα and wβ). Dropping the perpendicular from M to a triangle side gives the inradius r = A/s, which touches all three sides tangentially.

    Circumcircle of a Triangle

    The circumcenter M is found at the intersection of two perpendicular bisectors (e.g. on AB and BC). The compass circle centered at M with radius R = MA = MB = MC passes through all three vertices of the triangle.

      Determining the Center of a Circle

      Given a circle whose center is unknown (e.g. on a workpiece per DIN), two chords AB and CD are drawn. The intersection of their two perpendicular bisectors determines the exact circle center M.

      Construction step:
      Circle radius R 50 mm
      Angle of line a 28°
      Angle of line b 118°

      Workshop tip: To minimize intersection uncertainty, choose line b as close to perpendicular to line a as possible.

        Ellipse Construction from Two Circles

        Classical concentric-circle method (de La Hire / Pappus): Two concentric circles with diameters AB = 2a and CD = 2b are intersected by rays at angles φ. Parallels through the intersections E and F meet at exact ellipse points P(a·cos φ, b·sin φ).

        Construction step:
        Semi-major axis a 52 mm
        Semi-minor axis b 32 mm
        Active ray angle φ 45°

        With ray angle φ, the construction ray can be rotated dynamically through 360°. Every point satisfies the ellipse equation x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 exactly.

        Given: Axes AB and CD

          Ellipse Construction in a Parallelogram

          When a circle is projected obliquely (axonometric pictorials, isometry and dimetry per DIN ISO 5456-3), it appears as an ellipse inside a parallelogram. Halving, quartering, and eighthing the axes and projecting via an auxiliary semicircle of radius r = MC yields the curve points.

          Construction step:
          Semi-axis a (segment AM) 48 mm
          Semi-axis b (segment MC) 28 mm
          Shear angle ψ of axis CD 68°

          At a tilt angle of 90°, the parallelogram becomes a rectangle (orthographic ellipse). At 60° and 75°, isometric and dimetric ellipses are produced.

          Given: Parallelogram with axes AB and CD

            Spiral (Approximate Construction with a Compass)

            The four-center spiral approximates an Archimedean spiral (constant pitch a per 360° turn) by joining quarter-circle arcs. The central square ABCD of side length s = a/4 provides successive compass centers so that all transitions are strictly tangential (C¹-continuous).

            Construction step:
            Pitch (lead) a 48 mm

            Engineering applications: Screw conveyors, volute casings of centrifugal pumps and radial fans, spiral springs in measuring instruments.

            Given: Pitch a (lead per full revolution)
