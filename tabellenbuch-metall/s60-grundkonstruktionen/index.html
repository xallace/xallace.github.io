Parallel Line to a Segment

Stepping off the same compass radius r three times forms an equilateral parallelogram (rhombus) ACDP. The line through P and D is the desired parallel line g′ to AB.

Construction step:
Parallel spacing h 44 mm

Points P, A, and B can be dragged directly on the drawing sheet. Because r = AP, we have AC = PD = CD = r.

Given: Segment AB and point P on desired parallel g′

        Erecting a Perpendicular at Point P

        When P lies near the end of a segment or near the drawing edge, the perpendicular is constructed using a single compass radius r: via the equilateral triangle PAB (60°) and extending AB by r to C, Thales' theorem guarantees an exact 90° angle at P.

        Construction step:
        Side:
        Compass radius r 52 mm

            Dividing a Line Segment (Intercept Theorem)

            To divide segment AB into n equal parts without calculating or rounding errors, step off n equal intervals with a compass along an auxiliary ray from A, join the final mark to B, and draw parallels through all division marks.

            Construction step:
            Parts n:
            Auxiliary ray angle β 32°
            Compass step s along ray 16.5 mm

            Drag point B or endpoint 5′ of the auxiliary ray: by Thales' intercept theorem, the subdivisions on AB remain strictly equal for any angle β and any compass span s.

            Given: Segment AB to be divided into 5 equal parts.
