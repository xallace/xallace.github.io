        Example (Book p. 59)

        A measuring machine checks whether the roundness of a turned bushing lies within the required tolerance.

        The measured out-of-roundness was presumably caused by excessive clamping of the bushing in the chuck: the jaws (arrows) elastically compress the thin-walled bushing, and it is turned circular in this deformed state. After unclamping, it springs back, forming a high spot (lobe) at each jaw. With a 3-jaw chuck, this produces the characteristic triangular (trilobe) trace shown in the book.
