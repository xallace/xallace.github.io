Tangent Through Circle Point P

The radial line MP is extended past tangency point P. Striking a symmetric auxiliary circle around P marks segment AB, whose perpendicular bisector CD is the exact tangent at point P (∠(CD, MP) = 90°).

Construction step:
Circle radius R 48 mm
Angular position φ of P 55°
Aux. circle at P (r₁) 24 mm
Arcs at A & B (r₂ > r₁) 38 mm

Point P can be rotated directly along the circle on the drawing sheet. Because MP is the radius, the tangent is strictly perpendicular to it.

    Tangent from an External Point P to a Circle

    Segment MP is bisected. The circle centered at midpoint A with radius r = AM = AP (Thales circle) intersects the base circle at tangency points T (and T′). Since △MTP is inscribed in the semicircle, MT ⊥ PT (∠MTP = 90°).

    Construction step:
    Circle radius R 42 mm
    Distance MP 103 mm

    Point P can be dragged directly on the drawing sheet. The tangent length obeys the Pythagorean theorem: R2 + L2 = MP2.

      Corner Fillet (Rounding an Angle)

      Parallels to legs AS and BS are drawn at offset distance r. Their intersection M is the fillet center. Perpendiculars dropped from M onto the legs yield tangency transition points C and D (smooth C1 blend without a kink).

      Construction step:
      Interior angle α 72°
      Fillet radius r 36 mm

      Tangent setback distance is t = SC = SD = r / tan(α / 2). Vertex-to-center distance is SM = r / sin(α / 2).

        Connecting Two Circles with Tangent Arcs

        Two circles of radii r1 and r2 are smoothly joined by an inner arc (Ri, concave fillet) and an outer arc (Ra, convex envelope). Arc centers A and D are found by compass strikes with sum radii Ri + r and difference radii Ra − r (classic cam, flange, and connecting-rod contour).

        Construction step:
        Circle 1 radius r₁ 22 mm
        Circle 2 radius r₂ 32 mm
        Center distance a = M₁M₂ 96 mm
        Inner radius Ri (fillet) 55 mm
        Outer radius Ra (blend) 110 mm

        Tangency points B, C and E, F lie exactly on the lines joining the centers (collinearity proof of tangency).

        Given: Circle 1, Circle 2; radii R_i and R_a

          Regular Polygon Inscribed in a Circle (e.g. Pentagon)

          Bion's general approximate construction: vertical diameter AB is divided into n equal parts. An arc of radius r = AB around A yields pole points C and D at distance R·√3. Rays from C and D through the division marks (odd marks for odd n, even marks for even n) cut the polygon vertices on the circle.

            Hexagon & Dodecagon in Circumcircle

            Using the unchanged compass radius r = d/2, strike arcs from the endpoints of the diameter A and B → exact hexagon (s = r). By striking additional arcs from the horizontal points C and D, the circle is divided into 12 equal 30° sectors (dodecagon).
